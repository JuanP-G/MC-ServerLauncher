How to host a free Minecraft server without port forwarding

For friends to join your server you would normally have to forward ports on your router, which isn't always possible or wanted. A tunnel makes it unnecessary: your server reaches the Internet through a public address and you never touch your network settings.

Updated October 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Why you normally have to forward ports

Your router shares a single public address among everything at home and blocks whatever arrives from outside. For a friend to join you would have to redirect port 25565 to the server computer, and that fails if your provider shares one IP between several customers (CGNAT) or if you can't access the router. A tunnel avoids all of that: your PC opens a connection to the tunnel service, and that service receives your friends and hands the connection over.

Hosting it yourself is free: the server runs on your computer, you pay no monthly fee and there are no plan limits on players or mods. In return it is only available while your PC is on and the server is started.

Step by step with MC-ServerLauncher

  1. 1

    Create your server

    Install the app (Windows, Linux or macOS), click “New” and “Create a new one”, pick the type and version, tick “I accept the Minecraft EULA” and create the server. If you want mods, follow the modded server guide first.

  2. 2

    Connect your Playit account

    Create a free account at playit.gg. In the app, open “Tunnels” and paste the one-time setup code Playit gives you. The app creates the tunnel and runs the Playit agent for you: you install nothing else.

  3. 3

    Turn the tunnel on for your server

    In the server settings, under “Network and access”, turn on “Internet tunnel (Playit.gg)”.

  4. 4

    Start the server and copy the address

    Click “Start”. The public address appears on its own within seconds; click “Copy” and send it to your friends.

  5. 5

    Your friends join

    In Minecraft Java, they go to “Multiplayer”, “Add Server” and paste the address. They must use the same Minecraft version as the server.

Tip

If something is laggy, click “Test connection” on the Playit card: it measures your server, your Internet and the tunnel separately and tells you in one sentence where the delay comes from.

What limits this method

  • The server is only available while your PC is on and the app is open. To make it use nothing when nobody is playing, see how to make a Minecraft server stop itself when it is empty.
  • Your upload speed decides how many players you can handle well: each one constantly sends and receives data.
  • Playit's free plan has its own conditions; check its website for the current ones.
  • If you share the address, turn on the whitelist so only the people you choose can join.

FAQ

Common questions

How do I make a free Minecraft server to play with my friends?

Download the official server from Mojang (or let the app do it for you: see how to download the Minecraft server), run it on your computer and share it through a tunnel such as Playit. MC-ServerLauncher does all of that in a few clicks and costs nothing.

Is hosting a Minecraft server really free?

Yes, if you host it on your own computer. MC-ServerLauncher is free and open source, the official Minecraft server downloads for free and Playit has a free plan. The only thing you spend is the electricity of keeping the PC on.

Do I need to forward ports on my router?

No. The Playit tunnel receives your friends and hands the connection over, so you never touch the router. It also works if your provider uses CGNAT, where forwarding ports directly isn't possible.

Is my home IP visible?

Your friends connect to the tunnel's address, not to your home IP, and the tunnel's address is the one you share.

Can Bedrock players join?

Yes, with a Paper or Purpur server and Geyser. The app sets it up from “Crossplay” in the server settings and creates the second tunnel Bedrock needs.

What if I prefer paid hosting?

A host is always on and doesn't depend on your PC or connection, but it costs a fee and usually limits RAM and mods. Hosting at home is free and the world stays on your disk; both are valid, depending on how much you play.
